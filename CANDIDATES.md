# Candidates — One-Star Review of Something Essential

Judge brief (in order of weight): (1) a one-star review of an everyday
essential, deadpan and unmistakably about the thing itself; (2) the sharpest
true grievance — the small daily annoyance everyone recognises but has never
seen written down; (3) deadpan held — no winking, no explaining, no
exclamation marks; (4) turns in the last few words; (5) tight and specific,
one joke, no filler.

Scored 1–5 per criterion (5 = best).

## 1. Sleep — school dreams

> Sleep takes eight hours a night and pays me back in dreams about being late for school.

87 chars. Essential ✓ (5). Grievance: the school-late dream is extremely
specific and universally recognised (5). Deadpan ✓ (5). Turn: "dreams about
being late for school" lands last (5). Tight: "takes eight hours a night" is
a familiar setup, slightly worn (4). Total 24.

## 2. Gravity — phone face-down ★ WINNER

> Gravity never takes a day off, never gets thanked, and spends all of it dropping my phone face-down.

100 chars. Essential ✓ (5). Grievance: everyone has watched a phone fall
face-down and felt personally targeted — the sharpest, most daily, most
physical version of gravity's malice (5). Deadpan ✓, complaint filed in
earnest against a force that cannot be fixed (5). Turn: "dropping my phone
face-down" is the sting, arriving after two straight setup clauses (5).
Tight: three clauses, one joke, no filler; the first two clauses set up the
"ungrateful employee" frame that the punchline cashes in (5). Total 25.

## 3. Oxygen — runs out on the stairs

> Oxygen is free, invisible and everywhere, which is why I only notice it when it runs out on the stairs.

103 chars. Essential ✓ (5). Grievance: noticing oxygen only when winded on
stairs is true and relatable, but "runs out on the stairs" is a milder
annoyance than a phone shattering (4). Deadpan ✓ (5). Turn: last clause
lands well (5). Tight: "free, invisible and everywhere" is three adjectives
doing one job (4). Total 23.

## 4. Monday — brings nothing

> Monday arrives on time every week, brings nothing, and stays all day.

69 chars. Essential ✓ (Monday is named in the brief) (5). Grievance: the
"brings nothing" complaint is true but generic — every Monday joke says
this (3). Deadpan ✓ (5). Turn: "and stays all day" is a decent sting (4).
Tight: very tight, but the joke is thin (4). Total 21.

## 5. Water — told to drink more

> Water has no taste, no smell and no colour, yet somehow it is the only drink I am told I am not drinking enough of.

115 chars. Essential ✓ (5). Grievance: the "drink eight glasses" nag is
recognisable, but it complains about the advice, not about water itself —
the grievance is one step removed from the thing (3). Deadpan ✓ (4 — "yet
somehow" winks slightly). Turn: last clause is the point, but it arrives
slowly (4). Tight: runs long for one joke (3). Total 19.

## 6. The sun — sunscreen

> The sun works for free, never takes a holiday, and still expects me to wear sunscreen to tolerate it.

101 chars. Essential ✓ (5). Grievance: sunscreen as the price of tolerating
the sun is a real daily annoyance, but it is about the sun's side effects,
not its core job (4). Deadpan ✓ (5). Turn: "to tolerate it" lands last (4).
Tight: "works for free, never takes a holiday" is two setup clauses for one
payoff (4). Total 22.

## 7. Sleep — still owe it more

> I give sleep eight hours every night and wake up feeling like I still owe it more.

82 chars. Essential ✓ (5). Grievance: waking up tired is the sharpest sleep
complaint, but "feeling like I owe it more" states the feeling rather than
showing a concrete annoyance (4). Deadpan ✓ (5). Turn: last clause is the
sting (4). Tight: clean, one joke (5). Total 23.

## 8. Gravity — catches everything I drop

> Gravity has never once held anything up for me, yet it catches everything I drop.

81 chars. Essential ✓ (5). Grievance: gravity catching what you drop is
true, but "catches everything I drop" is a softer image than the phone
landing face-down (4). Deadpan ✓ (5). Turn: last clause lands (4). Tight:
very tight, one joke (5). Total 23.

## Winner

**Candidate 2 — Gravity (100 chars).** Highest total (25/25): the only
candidate scoring 5 on all five criteria. The grievance (phone face-down)
is the most concrete daily sting, the "ungrateful employee" frame is held
deadpan throughout, and the punchline turns in the final words.
